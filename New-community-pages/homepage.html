Rooted here

A coast shaped
over time.

The story of Bonita Springs and Estero stretches from the Calusa and the Estero River to the connected communities of today.

Before 1900 · Bonita Springs

First communities + Survey

People lived here thousands of years before European contact, and the Calusa built thriving communities around Southwest Florida's estuaries. In the 1870s, federal surveyors mapped the upper Imperial River area; the settlement that followed became known as Survey.

1882–1910 · Estero

Life along the river

Early homesteaders farmed citrus along the Estero River and shipped harvests toward the Gulf. The Koreshan Unity began settling here in 1894, establishing homes, workshops, schools and cultural spaces along the river.

1901–1936 · Bonita Springs

A town finds its name

Survey gained a post office in 1901 and was later renamed Bonita Springs. The railroad arrived in the early 1920s, the Tamiami Trail opened in 1928, and the Everglades Reptile Gardens—today's Wonder Gardens—welcomed visitors in 1936.

1904–20th century · Estero

The Koreshan legacy

The Koreshans incorporated a large Town of Estero in 1904; it was abolished in 1907. Their community gradually declined, but surviving structures and riverside grounds remain preserved at Koreshan State Park.

1940s–1999 · Bonita Springs

A modern coastal city

Air conditioning, a nearby airport, I-75 and the U.S. 41 bypass helped turn a quiet town into a growing coastal community. After decades of expansion, residents voted to incorporate Bonita Springs again in 1999.

2001–2015 · Estero

A village again

Local residents organized to guide Estero's rapid growth and protect its identity. In 2014, 86 percent of voters supported incorporation; the Village of Estero was established on December 31, with its first council seated in 2015.

Today · Bonita Springs

Old Florida, renewed

Downtown revitalization around Old 41 and the Imperial River is bringing new energy to the city while landmarks such as the Liles Hotel, Shangri-La Springs and Wonder Gardens keep local history visible.

Today · Estero

Growth with a sense of place

Estero continues to balance neighborhood growth, regional access and village-scale design with the Estero River, preserved open space and historic places at the center of its identity.
